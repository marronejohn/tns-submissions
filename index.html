Each report pairs the last pre-event baseline epoch with the first significant brightening, and states the OGLE EWS public fit, the timescale and impact parameter, and the case for a dark (stellar-remnant or black-hole) lens where the timescale supports one. Two events were followed up on 20 November with light-curve model comparison posted as reporter comments on their TNS pages: those two are best read as luminous binary lenses rather than dark lenses. The remaining five still stand as dark-lens candidates pending classification. Each object page also carries the time-reveal light-curve animation and the SkyMapper space-view companion attached to the report.

The two follow-up notes, 20 November

On AT 2026akez the follow-up found the significant non-PSPL structure well described by a luminous binary lens: a clean close/wide degeneracy (close s ≈ 0.50, q ≈ 0.42; wide s ≈ 1.90, q ≈ 0.19), preferred over the binary-source alternative by Δχ² ≈ 43, with the companion in the stellar/brown-dwarf mass-ratio range. On AT 2026akfd the same model comparison gives a binary-lens best fit at q ≈ 0.15 with a comparable binary-source solution, and the Gaia DR3 source (4090466448371776128) shows RUWE 2.07 and astrometric excess noise at 18.9σ, consistent with an astrometrically binary source. Both events remain plausible ongoing microlensing events; only the dark-lens interpretation was revised.
